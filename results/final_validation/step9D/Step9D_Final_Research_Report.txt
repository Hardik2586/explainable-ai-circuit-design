FINAL RESEARCH REPORT
Explainable AI Framework for Electronic Circuit Understanding
and Design Optimization
Common-Emitter BJT Amplifier
===============================================================

1. RESEARCH OBJECTIVE

The objective of this research is to develop an explainable, physics-grounded surrogate framework for understanding and optimizing an electronic circuit. The framework learns relationships between circuit design parameters and amplifier gain while retaining SPICE as the physical ground truth.

2. METHODOLOGY

Problem formulation:
The research addresses explainable surrogate modeling and design optimization of a Common-Emitter BJT amplifier. The objective is not to replace SPICE, but to create a data-driven assistant that learns relationships between circuit parameters and amplifier gain.

Dataset generation:
The canonical dataset contains 660 physically valid circuit configurations. The nine design variables are R1, R2, RC, RE, RL, Cin, Cout, Ce and VCC, with Gain_dB as the prediction target.

Physical validation:
Each canonical circuit was evaluated using SPICE. Operating point quantities including VCE and VCB were extracted and used to establish circuit-aware validity criteria.

Machine learning:
A classical LSBoost ensemble was used as the baseline model. A feed-forward deep neural network was then trained using the same canonical feature space and evaluated using a fixed held-out test set and five-fold cross-validation.

Explainable AI:
MATLAB SHAP analysis was used to quantify the contribution of individual circuit parameters to surrogate predictions. SHAP interaction analysis was further used to identify important parameter interactions.

Optimization:
The trained DL surrogate was embedded in constrained optimization to generate candidate amplifier designs. Optimization variables remained within dataset-derived bounds.

Circuit-aware validation:
Candidate designs were verified using SPICE. The selected operating-point constraints were VCE >= 1.0 V and VCB >= 0.5 V. SPICE remained the final physical authority.

Robustness:
Robustness was evaluated using 300 tolerance-perturbed SPICE simulations involving the specified resistor, capacitor and supply-voltage variations.

Generalization:
Optimization generalization was evaluated by comparing DL predictions against SPICE results and by examining transformed feature-space distance and standardized feature deviations.

3. DATASET

The canonical dataset contains 660 physically valid circuit configurations with 10 columns. The investigated gain range is 9.412 to 51.445 dB.

4. MODEL PERFORMANCE

The deep-learning model achieved R2=0.9007, RMSE=2.0978 dB and MAE=1.5673 dB on the held-out test set. The corresponding Pearson and Spearman correlations were 0.9507 and 0.9440.

Five-fold cross-validation produced R2=0.9174 +/- 0.0166.

Compared with LSBoost, the DL model improved R2 by 0.1140, reduced RMSE by 31.77% and reduced MAE by 38.45%.

5. EXPLAINABLE AI

The DL and LSBoost global SHAP rankings showed a Spearman correlation of 0.8667 and Pearson correlation of 0.9404. The R1-R2 interaction had a SHAP interaction value of 0.6144 and was further examined using SPICE validation.

6. OPTIMIZATION

The Low case improved by +6.7528 dB according to SPICE.
The Medium case improved by +4.2647 dB according to SPICE.
The High case changed by -0.5866 dB according to SPICE, despite a predicted improvement of +3.8906 dB.

7. CIRCUIT-AWARE VALIDATION

All three final designs satisfied VCE >= 1.0 V and VCB >= 0.5 V and were physically valid according to SPICE verification.

8. ROBUSTNESS

300 tolerance-perturbed SPICE simulations were performed. All tested simulations remained physically valid. The robustness result is limited to the specified perturbation ranges.

9. GENERALIZATION AND LIMITATIONS

The optimization prediction errors were 4.1133, 1.6633 and 2.1442 dB for the Low, Medium and High cases, respectively.

The High case demonstrates an important limitation: the surrogate predicted +3.8906 dB improvement while SPICE measured -0.5866 dB. Consequently, the learned model should be regarded as a surrogate and candidate-generation mechanism rather than a replacement for physical circuit simulation.

10. CONTRIBUTION

The principal contribution is an integrated framework connecting physics-grounded circuit data generation, surrogate modeling, explainability, constrained optimization, direct SPICE verification and robustness evaluation. The framework explicitly preserves the distinction between learned model behavior and physical circuit behavior.

11. CONCLUSION

This research developed an explainable surrogate-assisted framework for understanding and optimizing a Common-Emitter BJT amplifier. The framework was constructed around SPICE as the physical ground truth and used machine learning and deep learning models as computational surrogates. On a canonical dataset of 660 physically valid circuit configurations, the deep-learning model achieved a held-out test R2 of 0.9007, RMSE of 2.0978 dB and MAE of 1.5673 dB. Five-fold cross-validation produced an R2 of 0.9174 +/- 0.0166, supporting the stability of the learned surrogate on the investigated dataset.

Explainability analysis showed strong agreement between the global feature rankings of the deep-learning and LSBoost models, with a SHAP rank Spearman correlation of 0.8667. The R1-R2 interaction was identified as an important learned interaction and was further examined using direct SPICE sweeps. This establishes a link between model-level explanation and physical circuit validation without equating SHAP values with physical sensitivity coefficients.

The surrogate-assisted optimization stage produced positive SPICE-verified improvements of +6.7528 dB and +4.2647 dB for the Low and Medium representative cases, respectively. The High case, however, produced a SPICE change of -0.5866 dB despite a predicted improvement of +3.8906 dB. This result is a critical limitation and demonstrates why the learned surrogate cannot be treated as a replacement for physical circuit simulation.

All three final candidates satisfied the selected circuit-aware operating-point constraints of VCE >= 1.0 V and VCB >= 0.5 V. Furthermore, 300 tolerance-perturbed SPICE simulations remained physically valid. Therefore, the results support the framework as a physics-grounded, explainable and surrogate-assisted design methodology for the investigated Common-Emitter amplifier, while also establishing clear boundaries on its predictive and optimization generalization.
